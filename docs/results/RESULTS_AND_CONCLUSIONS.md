# HAB Forecast System: Results and Conclusions Section

## Results

### Model Performance and System Capabilities

The HAB forecast system achieves exceptional predictive accuracy with species-specific XGBoost models ranging from F1 scores of 0.943 to 0.999 across nine target species (Figure 1). The spatial-temporal approach successfully addressed the critical class imbalance problem, reducing positive class rates from 18.4% in naive temporal labeling to 1.7% through cell-specific risk prediction. All models exceeded the 94% F1 threshold for operational early warning systems, with Chattonella antiqua achieving near-perfect classification (F1 = 0.999), followed by Cochlodinium polykrikoides (F1 = 0.997) and Heterosigma akashiwo (F1 = 0.995).

**Figure 1. Species-Specific Model Performance Analysis**
Species-specific F1 scores demonstrating the predictive accuracy of XGBoost models for nine HAB species. Each bar represents the test set F1 score for individual species models, with colors indicating performance tiers. Chattonella antiqua (F1=0.999), Cochlodinium polykrikoides (F1=0.997), and Heterosigma akashiwo (F1=0.995) show the highest predictive accuracy. The consistently high performance (>0.94) across all species demonstrates the robustness of the spatial-temporal modeling approach and comprehensive feature engineering pipeline. The superior performance represents a significant advancement over traditional HAB prediction methods, establishing operational viability for early warning systems.

The system demonstrates remarkable scalability across three deployment configurations: Ultra-Fast Demo (30 seconds, 100K records), Smart Sampling (15 minutes, 2M records achieving 95% of full-dataset accuracy), and Batch Processing (2 hours, 12M records at 5km resolution). Learning curve analysis reveals continued performance improvement with larger datasets, while model stability analysis shows decreasing cross-validation standard deviation, confirming improved reliability at scale (Figure 2). Processing efficiency scales from 200,000 records/minute to comprehensive regional coverage, with uncertainty quantification providing essential confidence intervals for operational decision-making.

### Environmental Drivers and Temporal Patterns

Feature importance analysis reveals distinct environmental driver patterns across HAB species, with temperature emerging as the dominant driver for Chattonella species (importance: 0.61), salinity variations controlling Heterosigma akashiwo (importance: 0.56), elevation changes affecting Prorocentrum species (importance: 0.53), and complex nutrient dependencies governing Noctiluca (importance: 0.45) (Figure 3). These species-specific environmental niches validate ecological understanding while providing quantitative frameworks for risk assessment.

Temporal analysis of HAB events from 2017-2023 reveals critical seasonal and interannual patterns essential for predictive modeling (Figure 4). Summer months demonstrate peak activity with July recording 147 events, while annual trends exhibit significant variability including notable peaks in 2021. Species frequency analysis confirms Heterosigma akashiwo as the most prevalent species (12.9% of events), followed by Karenia mikimotoi (11.2%), with typical bloom persistence of 10-20 days (mean: 19.6 days) providing crucial parameters for early warning system design.

## Conclusions

### Main Findings and Contributions

This research presents four breakthrough achievements in harmful algal bloom prediction: (1) Spatial-temporal class imbalance solution reducing positive rates from 18.4% to 1.7%, enabling robust machine learning training with operational granularity; (2) Period-aware multi-source data integration framework successfully combining seven years of satellite, atmospheric, and oceanographic data; (3) Species-specific modeling achieving >94% F1 scores across nine HAB species, representing unprecedented accuracy in marine ecology applications; (4) Production-scale deployment architecture providing immediate operational capabilities across three operational tiers.

The HAB forecast system delivers immediate operational value through 24-48 hour early warning capabilities with species-specific risk assessment, cell-level (5-20 km) spatial resolution for targeted response strategies, and uncertainty quantification supporting risk-based decision frameworks. The comprehensive feature engineering pipeline incorporates temporal, spatial, biogeochemical, and interaction features, while the period-aware collection system with intelligent fallbacks ensures data continuity across varying source availability. Integration of confidence intervals, cross-validation stability metrics, and SHAP-based interpretability provides transparency required for operational environmental monitoring systems.

This research demonstrates effective integration of advanced machine learning and remote sensing for complex environmental challenges, providing a template for translating environmental research into operational management tools. The system's success in the Seto Inland Sea establishes proof-of-concept for global HAB monitoring enhancement, contributing to marine ecosystem protection, food security, and sustainable aquaculture development.

### Future Directions

The established model framework enables targeted extensions: (1) Ensemble integration combining species-specific XGBoost models with deep learning architectures for enhanced temporal sequence modeling; (2) Multi-species interaction modeling to capture competitive dynamics and co-occurrence patterns within the existing feature engineering pipeline; (3) Extended temporal forecasting horizons beyond 48 hours through recursive prediction and uncertainty propagation; (4) Enhanced feature engineering incorporating satellite-derived phytoplankton functional types and advanced biogeochemical indicators; (5) Transfer learning adaptation for rapid deployment to new marine regions using the proven spatial-temporal architecture.
